## First pass without assistance

1. Name the two proposed releases and who can inspect each.
2. Make a row for each important component. Include version, provenance, actor or role, governing record if known, proposed act, and unresolved question. Split assets when evidence differs.
3. Attach at least one plausible question about each protection mechanism. For one component, explain why two categories could matter without answering the same question.
4. Distinguish documented access from a right to redistribute. Identify a release hold and one reversible next step. Do not claim you know who owns everything.
5. Write a memo of 150–250 words for the designated review owner. Include date, U.S. scope, evidence IDs, disclosure timing, unresolved facts and proposed next decision.

## Feedback and revision

No account is needed. For the paper route, ask: Which decision did I actually name? Which row has a conclusion wider than its evidence? Which exact record would change that conclusion? Could I remove one suspect asset without changing the other decision?

For an optional model route, use only these invented records and a short excerpt of your draft. Ask: “Point to one conclusion broader than its cited evidence, say which record it relies on, and suggest a question that would resolve it. Do not infer legal clearance or add facts.” Check each suggestion against the record. Either way, submit a short revision log: first claim, evidence checked, change or reason to keep it, and feedback source (paper or tool). If you used a tool, disclose the name and the specific role it played. Use it only in this optional revision step and only when instructor rules allow it.

## Unaided transfer: Harbor Hours kiosk

A different fictional team has written permission to display a map in one in-person class. It now wants to put the layered map file in a public template archive. A recording of a public demonstration already showed a technical feature that the team had treated as private. The team also wants to review a possible patent filing in more than one country.

Without a tool or the key, identify the proposed change of use, one gap in permission evidence, two disclosure facts worth recovering, and one question to refer before releasing the archive. Give a step the team can take immediately without deciding legal rights. Do not assume removing the recording undoes the disclosure.
